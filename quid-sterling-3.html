How starting a US company from abroad works

The process is mostly online. Timelines depend on the state, the IRS and your bank, so treat any "done in 24 hours" promise with care.

  1. Choose your structure and state

    Most solo founders and small online businesses choose an LLC. If you plan to raise money from investors, a C-Corporation, usually in Delaware, is the common choice. The state affects your yearly fees, not where you can sell.

  2. Register the company and appoint a registered agent

    Every US company needs a registered agent with a physical address in its state to receive official letters. Formation services usually include this. Once the state accepts your filing, your company legally exists.

  3. Get your EIN

    The Employer Identification Number is your company's tax ID. Banks, Stripe and marketplaces will ask for it. You don't need a US Social Security Number to get one, but without one the application isn't done online and can take a few weeks.

  4. Open a US business bank account

    Several US banks and fintech providers open accounts remotely for non-resident owners. Approval isn't guaranteed and depends on your business, your documents and the provider's checks.

  5. Keep up with filings every year

    Most states want an annual report or tax, and the IRS has its own forms for foreign-owned companies, even ones with no income. Missing them can cost far more than filing on time.

Is a US company right for you?

It's a great fit for some businesses and an expensive distraction for others. Be honest with yourself here.

Often a good fit if you

  • Sell software, digital products or services to US customers
  • Need Stripe, US marketplaces or US payment platforms
  • Plan to raise money from US investors
  • Want a company that US clients recognise and trust

Probably not worth it if you

  • Only sell in your home country, where a local company is simpler
  • Hope it will remove tax in your home country (it usually won't)
  • Aren't ready to handle yearly US filings or pay someone to
  • Have a business that needs a US licence or physical premises

Common questions

Can I own a US company if I'm not a US citizen or resident?

Yes. US states let non-residents form and own LLCs and corporations. Owning a company doesn't give you the right to live or work in the US, though. That's a separate immigration matter.

Do I need to travel to the US?

Usually not. Formation and the EIN can be handled remotely, and several banking providers onboard non-residents online. A few traditional banks still ask for an in-person visit.

Do I need an SSN or ITIN?

Not to form the company or get an EIN. Some banks and payment providers may ask for extra ID, and you may need an ITIN later for certain personal tax situations.

Will I pay US tax?

It depends on your structure and whether your income is connected to business activity in the US. Many foreign-owned LLCs with no US activity owe little or no US income tax but still have filing duties. You'll usually still owe tax in your home country. Get proper advice.

What ongoing costs should I expect?

Typically a state annual report or franchise tax, a registered agent renewal and tax filing help. Amounts vary a lot by state, so compare before you choose.

What happens if I miss the yearly filings?

States can fine or dissolve your company, and the IRS penalty for a missing Form 5472 starts at $25,000. Set reminders or use a service that handles compliance.
